# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

OctoAcme’s project management framework centers on clear roles, defined workflows, and lightweight, repeatable processes designed to drive consistent execution across teams. Projects begin with a well-structured initiation phase, requiring a Project One-pager that outlines the problem, goals, metrics, key stakeholders, and early risks. Each project is assigned a dedicated Project Manager (PM) and Product Manager (PdM), with developers, QA, and supporting roles brought in as needed. The PM oversees scheduling, risk management, and cross-team communication, while the PdM prioritizes the backlog and defines success outcomes. These personas are empowered to ensure both delivery and business value, with predefined responsibilities and communication cadences ensuring alignment and accountability at each project stage.

Workflows at OctoAcme are built on transparency and iteration. Approved projects move from planning—where deliverables are broken into prioritized, well-scoped backlog items—to execution, where work is tracked via project boards (e.g., GitHub Projects) with standardized columns like Backlog, In Progress, QA, and Done. Pull request (PR) guidelines emphasize small, reviewable changes linked to tracked issues and run through automated CI pipelines for tests and linting. Each PR requires peer review and must meet explicitly defined acceptance criteria before merging. The execution phase is marked by daily standups, weekly syncs, and regular demos or reviews to surface blockers early and ensure progress toward milestones.

Communication is woven throughout the process. Weekly syncs between PMs and PdMs, twice-weekly delivery team standups, and monthly stakeholder updates maintain cross-functional awareness. Critical information—including risk registers, issue escalation paths, and decision logs—is documented and easily accessible. Risk management is proactive: risks are logged, assessed for likelihood and impact, and mitigation plans are continually updated and reviewed at regular intervals. When incidents or blockers arise, clear escalation paths ensure fast triage—escalating from team-level to PM, product, and ultimately sponsor if needed.

Quality assurance is integral rather than an afterthought. Every new feature or fix is expected to include accompanying unit, integration, and where needed, end-to-end tests. Manual QA augments automated checks for high-importance releases. Security scans are mandatory in the CI pipeline, and releases follow a disciplined checklist—from deployment windows and backups to smoke tests and rollback plans. After every sprint, release, or incident, teams run retrospectives to surface learnings, capture actionable improvements, and celebrate wins. These cycles of execution, reflection, and improvement foster a culture of accountability, continuous learning, and high-quality delivery at OctoAcme.
